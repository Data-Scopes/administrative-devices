## The unit: an item

Most resolutions hold one matter and one decision, but some hold several. The unit we classify is the **item**: one
proposition with its decision. From 1638 on there are 663,435 items in 616,128 resolutions.

## What the decision does

Each item gets one or more **action labels**. The labels are grounded in an existing archival typology of decisions
(Thomassen's functional typology), which sorts them into preliminary steps, decision-making and final decisions.

| Label | The assembly… |
|---|---|
| refer for advice | hands the matter to deputies or a committee to examine and report |
| commit deputies | appoints deputies to deal with the matter |
| send for disposal | sends the matter to another body to be dealt with |
| seek consent | asks the provinces or another party for their consent |
| hold pending | keeps the matter in suspense |
| write to | decides to write a letter |
| appoint | appoints someone to an office |
| grant / deny | grants or refuses a request |
| refer petitioner | refers the petitioner elsewhere |
| thank, acknowledge | thanks or takes note |
| order | gives an order |
| amend resolution | changes an earlier resolution |
| no action | records that no resolution was taken |

For example, *gestelt sal worden in handen vande heeren … om … daer van rapport te doen* ("shall be put in the hands of
the deputies … to report on it") is *refer for advice*. A passport request is typically granted, sent for disposal, and
answered by letter: three labels on one item.

## What the proposition contains

The proposition is split into clauses, and each clause gets a **function**: opening, sender, document type, date and
place, summary, request, reference to another document, or enclosure. Following the line *functions → formulas →
forms*, these functions are expressed by recurring formulas, and those formulas come close to the fields of a later
printed form.

## Over time

All results are reported per period, using periods drawn from the style of the registers themselves. The registers
changed over 220 years, and a method that works for one period may fail in another.
